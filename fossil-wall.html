The Fossil Wall

This page is a filled-in example, not a real team's station — it shows what a finished page should look like once every placeholder is replaced. The stone cladding in the Briger Hall lobby contains visible marine fossils, evidence that this building material formed at the bottom of an ancient sea long before it became a wall. Look closely near the entrance and you can find shell and coral shapes pressed into the rock.

3 Working Toward the UN Sustainable Development Goals

This feature connects most directly to:

SDG 4 — Quality Education SDG 11 — Sustainable Cities & Communities

The fossil wall turns an everyday building material into a hands-on geoscience lesson, building public science literacy (SDG 4) without a museum or classroom. Using naturally quarried stone that happens to carry visible fossils also reflects how building material choices connect to the historical and natural character of a place, supporting SDG 11's emphasis on protecting cultural and natural heritage in the built environment.
